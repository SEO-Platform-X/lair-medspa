Trust and safety, in plain English

What can go wrong when a vendor doesn't have these

Your marketing vendor logs into your Google profile, answers your patient reviews and edits your website. If they get it wrong, your practice carries the risk.

A US-based team

Without it: your logins and patient reviews are handled by people outside US law. If something goes wrong, there is no one here you can hold accountable, and your data may already have left the country.

With us: your account is run by our US-based team in Florida, New York, Washington and California.

HIPAA compliance and a signed BAA

Without it: a review reply that confirms someone is your patient can be a HIPAA violation, and you are the one fined. Federal penalties in 2026 run up to $73,011 per violation and up to $2.19 million a year for repeat violations of the same rule.

With us: we sign a Business Associate Agreement, so we are legally bound to protect patient information, and every reply follows HIPAA rules.

Insurance coverage

Without it: if a vendor's mistake leads to a data breach, a lawsuit or a suspended Google profile, an uninsured vendor can't pay for the damage. The cost lands on you.

With us: $750K in professional liability and cyber coverage stands behind the work on your account.

AES-256 encryption

Without it: if a vendor is hacked, your passwords, patient reviews and business data can be read by whoever took them.

With us: AES-256 is bank-level security. It's the same encryption standard banks use and the US government approves for top secret information. Stolen data stays unreadable.

ADA accessibility

Without it: a vendor that edits your website without accessibility standards can expose you to ADA lawsuits from visitors who use screen readers or can't use a mouse. Healthcare websites are a common target.

With us: the work we do on your site is built to be ADA accessible.

For public companies, PE-backed groups and multi-location brands

Your vendor's security becomes your disclosure problem

  • Public companies must report a material cyber incident to the SEC within four business days of deciding it's material. That includes incidents that happen at a vendor.
  • Every annual report (10-K) must describe how the company oversees cyber risk from outside vendors. An uninsured, offshore vendor with no BAA is hard to explain.
  • Private equity buyers review every vendor before an acquisition. Weak vendor security shows up as a red flag in diligence and can slow a deal or lower the price.

Why the activities alone don't work

  • Every competitor has the same listings. Synced directories are table stakes. They stop you losing points. They don't win you any.
  • AI recommends businesses other people talk about. News articles, real reviews, expert content and a website it can read. Tools don't produce any of that.
  • Software can't be accountable. A dashboard reports what it did. Nobody's job depends on whether you got more patients.
